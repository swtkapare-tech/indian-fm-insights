Daily market brief · Friday, 20 November 2026 close

Bank stocks carry Sensex to 83,919.71 and Nifty to 25,800.60 as the rupee breaks past 93 and gold slides 4.9%

Indian equities closed higher for a second straight session on Friday 20 November 2026, in the opposite direction to most of Asia. The Sensex rose 897.96 points, or 1.08%, to 83,919.71 and the Nifty 50 added 286.30 points, or 1.12%, to 25,800.60, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Business Standard, The Hindu, ET Now and Upstox. The Sensex touched 84,037.96 during the session, a gain of 1,016.21 points at its best. Breadth did not follow the benchmarks: 2,456 stocks fell against 1,582 that rose on the BSE, according to ET Now, and 18 of the 50 Nifty stocks ended lower, according to Upstox.

Banks and financials did most of the work. The Nifty Private Bank index rose 3.5%, Nifty Bank 2.5% and Nifty PSU Bank 1.2%, while the Nifty Bank index closed above 64,600 for the first time, according to ET Now and Upstox; Nifty IT added 1%, and Nifty Media fell about 2% with Metal and Consumer Durables down about 0.5% each. The rupee settled 65 paise stronger at 92.67 per dollar (provisional), its first close above 93 since 10 April, according to PTI and Reuters. IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,23,584 per 10 g, down ₹6,401 (-4.92%) from Thursday's benchmark, and 999 silver at ₹1,50,650 per kg, down ₹10,925 (-6.76%). Brent crude eased about 1.9% to roughly $90 a barrel, according to PTI and Reuters, and foreign investors bought ₹7,411.41 crore of Indian equities on Thursday, exchange data cited by PTI showed.

Why this matters to you: A session like this one is a lesson in reading several prices at once, because they moved for different reasons. The index gain came mostly from one group of shares, which is why 2,456 stocks fell while the Sensex rose; a headline index number hides that. The rupee and gold moved on the same day in opposite directions to each other, and both are quoted in the same currency you spend: a stronger rupee makes imports cheaper, and gold priced in rupees carries both the world price and the exchange rate inside it. Read each level with its date, its change and its own benchmark, and you can tell what actually moved from what merely made a headline. This page is education, not advice: it records what the market did on one dated session, and it names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
