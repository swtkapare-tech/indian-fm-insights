Daily market brief · Monday, 16 November 2026 close

Banks and FMCG lift Nifty to 25,291.10 and Sensex to 82,104.79 as foreign inflows return

Indian equities closed higher on Monday 16 November 2026, extending three weeks of gains, as banking and FMCG shares led and sustained foreign fund inflows and softer crude oil prices supported sentiment. The Sensex rose 512.34 points, or 0.63%, to 82,104.79 and the Nifty 50 gained 146.80 points, or 0.58%, to 25,291.10, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Moneycontrol, Zee Business, India Today and Upstox. The Sensex traded as high as 82,311.65 during the session and the Nifty as high as 25,337.60 before both gave back part of the gain, according to Upstox and Goodreturns.

Nifty Bank closed at 61,575.90, up 541.80 points or 0.89%, according to PTI and Upstox, and financial and FMCG shares carried the benchmark indices. Foreign institutional investors bought equities worth ₹5,736.55 crore on Friday, exchange data reported by PTI showed, and Reuters reported that the rebound in foreign equity inflows steadied the rupee, with one-month realised volatility easing to about 4% from 6% two weeks earlier. The rupee closed at 93.92 per dollar, 27 paise stronger than its previous close in PTI’s provisional print and about 0.3% higher in Reuters’ report. IBJA’s 16 November morning benchmark put 24K 999 gold at ₹1,34,380 per 10 g, down ₹1,429 from Friday’s evening print, and 999 silver at ₹1,69,280 per kg, down ₹8,720.

Why this matters to you: A close is a record of what buyers and sellers agreed on for one session, and nothing more. The Nifty is up 0.58% and the Sensex 0.63% on 16 November — those are facts about one day, not a signal about the next. Read a level together with its date and its change: a percentage without a date is a claim you cannot check. Learning to read an index close, a currency level and a bullion benchmark side by side is the skill this page teaches, and it is a skill for understanding the market, never a forecast of it. This page is education, not advice: it records what the market did on one dated session, and it names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
