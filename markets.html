Daily market brief · Friday, 13 November 2026 close

Three-day rally ends: Nifty 25,144.30, Sensex 81,592.45 as October inflation tops 6%

Indian equities closed lower on Friday 13 November 2026, snapping a three-session winning run, as auto, metal and realty shares led the decline. The Sensex fell 318.56 points, or 0.39%, to 81,592.45 and the Nifty 50 dropped 155.05 points, or 0.61%, to 25,144.30, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Moneycontrol, The Hindu BusinessLine, Zee Business and Upstox. The Sensex fell as much as 622.26 points to 81,288.75 during the session and the Nifty traded as low as 25,054.40 before recovering part of the loss, according to PTI and Upstox.

October consumer-price inflation, released after Thursday’s close at 6.09%, above the RBI’s 6% tolerance ceiling, kept expectations of a policy rate increase at the December MPC in play, PTI and Reuters reported, while firm crude oil prices and a stronger dollar added to the pressure; banking, auto and information-technology heavyweights saw profit-booking after the three-day rally, according to Zee Business and Moneycontrol. The rupee closed at 94.24 per dollar, about 20 paise weaker than Thursday’s 94.04, according to Reuters and Moneycontrol. IBJA’s 13 November PM benchmark, the session’s final print, put 24K 999 gold at ₹1,35,809 per 10 g, down ₹1,883 from the 12 November PM level, and 999 silver at ₹1,78,000 per kg, down ₹3,040.

Why this matters to you: A single session changes the price of what you already own, not the plan you made before it. A daily close is a fact about one day of trading — it is not a forecast, and it says nothing about what the next session will do. That is why this page dates every level and prints the change beside it: a number read on its own is easy to mistake for a signal.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
