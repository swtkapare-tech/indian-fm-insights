Daily market brief · 27 October 2026

Indices end flat, but the real action was under the surface

The 30-share BSE Sensex settled 8.08 points lower at 73,598.67 and the NSE Nifty 50 shed 3.45 points to close at 22,749.75, both moves well under 0.05%. It was an expiry session, so much of the intraday swing came from October futures and options rolling over rather than from fresh conviction. Underneath the flat headline the split was wide: bank and financial shares fell, while pharma, realty and media gained 2-3%, and the Nifty Midcap 100 rose about 1% with the Smallcap 100 up nearly 2.7%. Breadth stayed positive, with 2,455 NSE stocks advancing against 1,103 declining. Traders also kept one eye on crude near a six-month high and the other on the US Federal Reserve's policy decision later this week.

The rupee ended marginally stronger at 95.12 per dollar, after trading in a tight 95.09-95.24 band through a session in which the RBI was seen supporting the currency. Bullion cooled: the India Bullion and Jewellers Association put 999-purity gold at ₹1,41,082 per 10 g, down ₹628 from Monday's close, and 999 silver at ₹1,92,037 per kg, down ₹2,442. Softer metal prices arrive just as festive buying picks up, though retail jewellery rates still carry making charges and GST on top of the bullion rate. Foreign portfolio investors have pulled nearly $5 billion out of Indian equities this month, which has weighed on both the rupee and overall sentiment.

Why this matters to you: An index level tells you what the biggest companies did, not what your own holdings did. A day when the Nifty barely moves can still be a strong day for mid and small-cap funds, or a weak one for a bank-heavy portfolio — which is why it helps to look at what you actually own, and at why each fund is in your plan, rather than only at the headline number.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
