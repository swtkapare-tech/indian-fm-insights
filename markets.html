Daily market brief · Wednesday, 25 November 2026 close

Sensex rebounds 1,502.29 points to 83,938.28 and the Nifty 50 gains 469.60 to 25,819.25 as foreign inflows return and the rupee jumps to an eight-month high

Indian equities staged a sharp rebound on Wednesday 25 November 2026, a session after Monday's slide. The Sensex rose 1,502.29 points, or 1.82%, to close at 83,938.28, and the Nifty 50 added 469.60 points, or 1.85%, to 25,819.25, according to the closing data reported by PTI, Reuters and the exchanges' end-of-day figures carried by Moneycontrol, Business Standard and The Hindu BusinessLine. The Nifty opened at 25,658.35, ran up to 25,951.65 and held a low of 25,619 during the session; the Sensex opened at 83,436.89 and touched 84,498.21 at its best. Buying was broad rather than narrow, with banks, financials and fast-moving consumer goods shares leading and information technology shares also closing higher, even as Asian markets were mixed. Dealers put the move down to three things: renewed foreign portfolio inflows into Indian equities, softer crude oil prices, and the relief of a stronger rupee. Monday's fall, by contrast, had been profit-booking after a four-week rally.

The rupee was the day's other big number. It closed at 92.23 per US dollar, an eight-month high, according to Reuters' closing report and market quotes for the day; PTI's provisional close was 92.16, 69 paise stronger than Monday. Against the 92.92 level this page published for the previous session that is a gain of 0.69 for the rupee, or 0.74%. Bullion went the other way. IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,21,310 per 10 g, down ₹3,813 (3.05%) from the previous working session's morning fixing, and 999 silver at ₹1,43,522 per kg, down ₹9,419 (6.16%) — a 13-month low for silver, as a firm dollar and expectations of another US rate move kept pressure on precious metals. The next session is Thursday 26 November 2026.

Why this matters to you: A single strong day is one day's data, not a trend: the Nifty is back above 25,800, but that is simply where buyers and sellers met at 3:30 pm on Wednesday 25 November, and it says nothing about Thursday. This page is education, not advice: it records one dated session and names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return. Every figure here is checked against two independent sources dated the same session; where two sources disagree, the figure is dropped rather than estimated.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
