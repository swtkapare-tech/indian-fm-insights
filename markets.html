Daily market brief · Thursday, 26 November 2026

Sensex adds 390.31 points to 84,328.59 and the Nifty 50 gains 65.25 to 25,884.50 as information technology shares lead a second day of gains and the rupee slips on fresh US tariff proposals

Indian equities closed higher for a second straight session on Thursday 26 November 2026, though the day was less smooth than the headline suggests. The Sensex rose 390.31 points, or 0.46%, to 84,328.59 and the Nifty 50 added 65.25 points, or 0.25%, to 25,884.50, according to NSE closing data and the closing reports of PTI, carried by The Hindu, The Hindu BusinessLine and Rediff Moneynews, with Business Standard, NDTV Profit and Upstox carrying the identical two levels. Both indices are at roughly ten-month highs. The Sensex ran up as much as 613.46 points to 84,551.74 and the Nifty to 25,974.95 before profit-booking in the afternoon cut most of the intraday gain, and the Sensex ended the session 223 points below its best level. The buying was concentrated rather than broad: information technology, realty and metal shares led, while fast-moving consumer goods, auto, public-sector bank and media shares closed lower, and the Bank Nifty actually fell 167.95 points, or 0.26%, to 63,535.30. Dealers gave two reasons for the support: sustained foreign fund inflows into Indian equities and heavy buying in technology shares, with the broader market holding up better than the benchmarks.

The rupee was the drag. It settled at 92.44 per US dollar, 24 paise weaker on PTI's provisional closing figure, which measures the move from its own previous close of 92.20; against the 92.23 level this page published for the previous session that is a fall of 0.21, or 0.23%. Traders pointed to crude oil back above $95 a barrel and to the US proposing a 25% tariff on Indian goods, to be capped at 17% if a bilateral trade deal is agreed. Bullion moved the other way, and sharply. IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,22,982 per 10 g, up ₹1,672 (1.38%) from the previous working session's morning fixing, and 999 silver at ₹1,46,933 per kg, up ₹3,411 (2.38%). The next session is Friday 27 November 2026.

Why this matters to you: Two sessions of gains are still two sessions of data, not a trend: the Nifty is back near 25,900, but that is simply where buyers and sellers met at 3:30 pm on Thursday 26 November, and it says nothing about Friday. Note also that a rising index and a falling rupee can happen on the same day, because they are moved by different buyers. This page is education, not advice: it names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
