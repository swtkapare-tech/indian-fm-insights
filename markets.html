Daily market brief · Friday, 27 November 2026

Sensex slides 989.33 points to 83,339.26 and the Nifty 50 gives up 277.30 to 25,607.20 as information technology, auto and realty shares fall and crude oil above $98 a barrel weighs on sentiment

Indian equities closed sharply lower on Friday 27 November 2026, snapping a two-session winning run. The Sensex fell 989.33 points, or 1.17%, to 83,339.26 and the Nifty 50 fell 277.30 points, or 1.07%, to 25,607.20, according to NSE closing data and the closing reports of PTI, carried by The Hindu BusinessLine, Dailyworld and PTI's own wire, with Capital Market, Upstox, The Hawk and Moneycontrol carrying the identical two levels. The Nifty 50 opened slightly lower at 25,839.25, climbed to an intraday high of 25,909.15 and then slid to a low of 25,565.90, recovering a little into the close; the Sensex traced the same path down to 83,208.33 before steadying. The selling was led by information technology shares, down 1.88% as a sector, with realty down 1.43%, auto down 1.48%, private banks down 1.12% and fast-moving consumer goods down 0.93%; only metal, media and consumer durables closed higher. Dealers attributed the fall to profit-booking after two days of gains, to crude oil trading above $98 a barrel after fresh drone attacks in West Asia, and to continuing uncertainty over the India-United States trade agreement.

The broader market did not follow the benchmarks down: the Nifty Midcap 100 rose 0.11% to 61,847.40 and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rose 0.57% to 19,482.20, and more stocks on the NSE advanced than declined. The Bank Nifty fell 494.40 points, or 0.78%, to 63,040.90 and India VIX closed at 16.66. The rupee was the second drag: it settled at 92.98 (provisional) against the US dollar, 60 paise weaker than PTI's own previous close of 92.38, after opening at 92.44 and touching a low of 93.01, as higher crude prices and month-end dollar demand weighed on the currency. In bullion, IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,25,316 per 10 g, up ₹2,334 or 1.90%, and 999 silver at ₹1,53,275 per kg, up ₹6,342 or 4.32% — the AM fixing is used throughout so the comparison with the previous session stays like-for-like.

Why this matters to you: A single session's move says very little on its own: the Sensex is still only about 1.2% below the ten-month closing high it set on Thursday, and one day's fall or rise is noise next to the years over which an equity investment actually works. What a day like this does show is how quickly sentiment can turn on oil prices, currency moves and trade headlines — which is exactly why a long-horizon plan is built to be held through such days rather than traded around them. This page reports what happened; it is education, not advice.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
