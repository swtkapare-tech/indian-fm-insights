Daily market brief · Monday, 9 November 2026

Nifty ends at 25,044.65 and Sensex at 81,106.65 as banks and pharma offset an IT slide

Indian equities closed marginally higher on Monday, 9 November 2026, with the Sensex adding 107.25 points, or 0.13%, to 81,106.65 and the Nifty 50 rising 58.25 points, or 0.23%, to 25,044.65, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by The Hindu BusinessLine, Business Standard, Moneycontrol, PTI and Upstox. It was a volatile session that ended better than it traded: the Nifty fell to 24,838.75 and the Sensex to 80,519.31 during the day before both recovered into the close, according to Upstox's market wrap and PTI's closing report.

The buying came from public-sector bank and pharmaceutical shares while information-technology stocks slipped, according to the day's closing reports, and traders linked the broader mood to a correction in crude oil prices and returning foreign inflows. On the currency side, the rupee touched a six-month high of 93.69 per dollar before closing at 93.93, a shade weaker than Friday's 93.91, as state-run banks bought dollars, according to Reuters and PTI. Bullion is shown as pending here: IBJA had not published its 9 November benchmark when this page refreshed, and its last published fixing is still Friday 6 November's, so no gold or silver level is carried forward as today's close.

Why this matters to you: A session that ends 0.13% higher after trading sharply lower tells you about the path, not just the destination. The closing level, the intraday swing and the reason behind the buying are three different pieces of information, and reading them together is what turns a headline number into something you can learn from. One session, in either direction, says nothing about the next one.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
