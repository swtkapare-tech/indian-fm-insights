Daily market brief · Tuesday, 17 November 2026 close

Banks carry Nifty to 25,387.40 and Sensex to 82,532.19, their highest close since February

Indian equities closed higher on Tuesday 17 November 2026, a second straight session of gains, with banking shares doing most of the lifting. The Sensex rose 427.40 points, or 0.52%, to 82,532.19 and the Nifty 50 gained 96.30 points, or 0.38%, to 25,387.40, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, The Hindu BusinessLine, CNBC-TV18, Zee Business, Capital Market and Upstox. The Hindu BusinessLine and CNBC-TV18 both describe the close as the highest since late February, with the Nifty up about 10.1% and the Sensex about 10.8% so far in November as foreign portfolio inflows reached a record $9.53 billion for the month. The Sensex traded as high as 82,897.49 in the session, up 792.70 points at its best, according to PTI.

Banks led the move: the Nifty PSU Bank index rose 1.95%, Nifty Realty 1.94% and the Nifty Private Bank index 0.89%, and the Nifty Bank scaled a record intraday high of 62,442.20 before closing at a record, according to Capital Market and Zee Business. Information technology lagged, with the Nifty IT index down 0.92%, and 27 of the 50 Nifty stocks ended higher, according to Upstox. Brent crude for December settlement eased 13 cents to $92.27 a barrel, which lowers the import bill for a net oil importer. The rupee closed at 93.51 per dollar, 43 paise stronger than Monday's close in PTI's provisional print, while Reuters put the close at 93.47, its strongest in seven months. IBJA's 17 November morning benchmark put 24K 999 gold at ₹1,34,418 per 10 g, down ₹553 from Monday's evening print, and 999 silver at ₹1,68,950 per kg, down ₹1,301.

Why this matters to you: A close is a record of what buyers and sellers agreed on for one session, and nothing more. The Nifty is up 0.38% and the Sensex 0.52% on 17 November, their highest close since February &mdash; those are facts about one day, not a signal about the next. Read a level together with its date and its change: a percentage without a date is a claim you cannot check. Learning to read an index close, a currency level and a bullion benchmark side by side is the skill this page teaches, and it is a skill for understanding the market, never a forecast of it. <b>This page is education, not advice:</b> it records what the market did on one dated session, and it names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
