Daily market brief · Monday, 23 November 2026 close

Sensex closes 1,484 points lower as a four-week rally meets profit-taking, Nifty ends at 25,349.65

Indian equities closed sharply lower on Monday 23 November 2026, giving back part of a four-week rally. The Sensex fell 1,483.72 points, or 1.77%, to 82,435.99 and the Nifty 50 lost 450.95 points, or 1.75%, to 25,349.65, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Reuters, Zee Business, Business Today and The Hindu BusinessLine. Both indices opened higher and the Nifty touched 25,992.60 during the session, its highest level since February, before the selling took it down to a low of 25,343.85; the Sensex gave up more than 2,200 points from its intraday high. Banks, financials, metals and energy shares led the fall, and dealers attributed the move to profit-booking after the recent run rather than to a single domestic event, with a global sell-off in AI-linked technology stocks adding to the mood.

The rupee ended a five-session winning streak and settled at 92.92 (provisional) against the US dollar, 25 paise weaker than its previous close, after touching 92.44 in early trade, according to PTI and Reuters. IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,25,123 per 10 g, up ₹1,539 (1.25%) from the previous session's morning fixing, and 999 silver at ₹1,52,941 per kg, up ₹2,291 (1.52%), as bullion rebounded after last week's steep fall. Tuesday 24 November 2026 is a listed exchange holiday, so the next session is Wednesday 25 November 2026.

Why this matters to you: One down day after a four-week rally is ordinary market behaviour, not a signal. A closing level tells you what buyers and sellers agreed on at 3:30 pm on one dated session; it does not say what happens next. What the day does show is how a move travels: the same profit-booking hit banks, metals and energy together, while gold and silver rose and the rupee weakened, so four prices moved for four different reasons. This page is education, not advice: it records one dated session and names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
