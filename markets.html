    Daily market brief · Thursday, 12 November 2026 close

    Third straight gain, but only just: Nifty 25,299.35, Sensex 81,911.01

    Indian equities closed marginally higher on Thursday 12 November 2026, a third straight session of gains, as buying in realty, banking and media shares offset profit-booking in information technology, automobiles and fast-moving consumer goods. The Sensex rose 31.61 points, or 0.04%, to 81,911.01 and the Nifty 50 gained 44.30 points, or 0.18%, to 25,299.35, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Moneycontrol, The Hindu BusinessLine, Zee Business and Reuters. Both benchmarks opened lower and recovered within the first hour — the Nifty traded as high as 25,384.80 and as low as 25,142.95, and the Sensex between 82,325.61 and 81,576.15, according to PTI and Moneycontrol — before profit-booking at higher levels pared most of the advance.

    Sustained foreign fund inflows and lower crude oil prices supported the market, Reuters reported, though crude turned higher as the session wore on and equities gave up their intraday gains; traders were also waiting for October consumer-price inflation data due later in the day. The rupee touched 93.62 in early trade, its strongest level since April, before closing at 94.04 per dollar, about 35 paise weaker than Wednesday’s 93.69, according to Reuters and PTI. Bullion fell again: IBJA’s 12 November morning benchmark put 24K 999 gold at ₹1,38,294 per 10 g, down ₹2,511 from the 11 November morning level, and 999 silver at ₹1,81,999 per kg, down ₹4,496. Released after the close, October consumer-price inflation came in at 6.09%, above the RBI’s 6% tolerance ceiling and the highest since October 2024, with food inflation at 8.94%, according to the National Statistics Office — a figure this brief did not carry when it was written, and one that matters for the December MPC.

    Why this matters to you: A single session changes the price of what you already own, not the plan you made before it. A daily close is a fact about one day of trading — it is not a forecast, and it says nothing about what the next session will do. That is why this page dates every level and prints the change beside it: a number read on its own is easy to mistake for a signal.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
