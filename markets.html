Daily market brief · Friday, 6 November 2026

Nifty closes above 25,000 and Sensex tops 81,000 as the winning run stretches to a seventh session

Indian equities closed higher for a seventh straight session on Friday, with the Sensex adding 1,718.01 points, or 2.17%, to 81,028.02 and the Nifty 50 rising 493.75 points, or 2.01%, to 25,030.35, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by Capital Market, India Today and The Hindu BusinessLine. Private banks led the advance, with the Nifty Private Bank index up 3.22% and the Nifty Bank index up 2.27%, according to NSE data, while the India VIX rose 13.35% to 17.66 — a reminder that a rising index and a calmer market are not the same thing. The Nifty Midcap 150 slipped 0.09% and the Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 0.85%, so the gain was concentrated in the largest companies rather than spread across the market.

The trigger was money coming in rather than news coming out. Foreign institutional investors bought a further ₹13,749.82 crore of Indian shares on Friday, after a record ₹14,975 crore on Thursday, according to exchange data reported by The Hindu BusinessLine, and the rupee strengthened 56 paise to close at 93.91 per dollar — its strongest level since 22 April and its biggest single-day gain since 12 October, according to Moneycontrol and Business Standard. That happened even as the Nasdaq fell 5.52% overnight, its worst session since April 2025, on worries about the cost of artificial intelligence infrastructure. On the bullion side, the IBJA national benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,41,452 per 10 g, down ₹2,677 or 1.86%, and 999 silver at ₹1,82,868 per kg, down ₹4,432 or 2.37%, as reported by Inshorts and Dainik Jagran. Indian markets were closed on Saturday, 7 November, so these are Friday's closing levels.

Why this matters to you: A one-day gain tells you what happened in a single session, not what will happen next. A 2% index move can come from flows, from currency, or from a shift in global sentiment, and a seventh straight up-day does not make the eighth one predictable. Reading the level, the reason and the breadth together is the habit that matters more than any single day's number.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
