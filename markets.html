Daily market brief · Wednesday, 18 November 2026 close

Profit-taking takes Nifty to 25,227.95 and Sensex to 82,031.81 as consumer durables and metals slide

Indian equities closed lower on Wednesday 18 November 2026, giving back part of the previous session's gains. The Sensex fell 500.38 points, or 0.61%, to 82,031.81 and the Nifty 50 lost 159.45 points, or 0.63%, to 25,227.95, according to NSE and BSE closing data reported by PTI, Business Standard, Zee Business, Upstox and Reuters. The Sensex traded as low as 81,780.47 in the session, down 751.72 points at its worst, and the Nifty touched 25,141.80, according to PTI and Moneycontrol. Thirty-five of the 50 Nifty stocks ended lower, the Nifty Midcap 100 fell 1.22% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 0.35%, according to Upstox.

Consumer durables was the weakest sector, with the Nifty Consumer Durables index down 3.13%, followed by Nifty Metal at 2.46%, Nifty Auto at 1.35% and Nifty IT at 1.24%, according to Upstox; only PSU banks, media and pharma closed higher. Traders pointed to profit-taking after Tuesday's nine-month high and to selling in other Asian markets, while a dollar near a 19-month high and expectations of a US rate increase in December weighed on metal prices. Brent crude for January settlement eased to about $91 a barrel, according to Investing.com data cited by Upstox. The rupee closed at 93.44 per dollar, 7 paise stronger than Tuesday's 93.51, according to PTI and Reuters. IBJA's 12:00 IST benchmark put 24K gold at ₹1,31,505 per 10 g, down ₹2,913 (-2.17%), and 999 silver at ₹1,64,506 per kg, down ₹4,444 (-2.63%), as spot gold slipped below $3,800 an ounce.

Why this matters to you: A close is a record of what buyers and sellers agreed on for one session, and nothing more. The Nifty fell 0.63% and the Sensex 0.61% on 18 November, one day after both closed at nine-month highs — those are facts about one day, not a signal about the next. Read a level together with its date and its change: a percentage without a date is a claim you cannot check. Learning to read an index close, a currency level and a bullion benchmark side by side is the skill this page teaches, and it is a skill for understanding the market, never a forecast of it. This page is education, not advice: it records what the market did on one dated session, and it names no stock, no fund, no target and no expected return.

How to read this page

A daily market page is not a signal to act. The useful habit is to look at three things only: the direction of the index over weeks rather than hours, whether foreign money is buying or selling, and whether anything changed in interest rates or inflation. Everything else is noise dressed up as news. If you want the numbers without the drama, this page is for you — and every figure here is checked before it is published.
